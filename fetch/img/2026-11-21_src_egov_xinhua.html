Digital government advances worldwide, but gaps persist: UN survey

Source: Xinhua

Editor: huaxia

2026-11-20 05:43:30

UNITED NATIONS, Nov. 19 (Xinhua) -- Digital government services continue to improve worldwide, but gaps persist as artificial intelligence (AI) transforms public administration, according to a new UN survey.

The 2026 edition of the UN E-Government Survey, whose results were released Thursday, shows that the Republic of Korea leads the digital government ranking of the 193 UN member states plus the State of Palestine.

Denmark, Singapore, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Iceland, Britain, Estonia, Finland and Australia are among the top 10 countries, which score the highest when it comes to the scope and quality of online services, telecommunication infrastructure and human capital.

The top-performing very high rating class has expanded from 18 to 21 countries, with Ireland, Kazakhstan and Poland joining for the first time and the United States returning. More broadly, 151 of the 194 countries assessed now belong to the high or very high E-Government Development Index (EGDI) groups, compared with 138 in 2024.

The global average EGDI increased by 4.6 percent, from 0.6382 in 2024 to 0.6675 in 2026. Among the 35 countries that changed EGDI group, 28 moved upward and seven downward. Yet 10 countries remain in the low group, reflecting persistent structural constraints and the need for differentiated support, according to the survey.

All regions improved. Europe remains the regional leader, followed by Asia, the Americas, Oceania and Africa. Oceania recorded the strongest relative gain, at 15.5 percent, followed by Africa at 10.5 percent.

Nevertheless, approximately 87 percent of Africa's population lives in countries below the global EGDI average. Least developed countries improved their average EGDI by 16 percent, but substantial gaps persist.

Despite overall progress, some 1.95 billion people, or about a quarter of the world's population, live in countries below the global EGDI average -- 53.3 million more than in 2024.

At the local level, digital government services continued to advance. The number of cities in the very high Local Online Service Index (LOSI) group increased from 42 in 2024 to 68 in 2026. Six cities achieved the highest LOSI score: Dubai, Madrid, Manama, Riyadh, Sao Paulo and Vienna.

The 2026 assessment covers 194 cities across 101 indicators, examining the availability and quality of local online services.

The survey also points to an emerging transition toward AI-enabled and potentially agentic public administration, where AI systems could coordinate workflows, anticipate people's needs, and support more proactive services.

The survey stresses that public officials must retain responsibility for decisions, supported by trusted data, cybersecurity, human oversight and auditable safeguards. Without these foundations, existing inequalities could deepen into a new AI divide.

At the launch of the survey results, UN Assistant Secretary-General for Economic and Social Affairs Navid Hanif highlighted four messages: There is a need to accelerate digitalization while transforming service delivery; The digital divide is becoming a development divide; Digital transformation must reach local communities; AI could fundamentally change public administration.

Hanif said the fundamental principle here is trust.

"Technology can enable transformation. Only transparent, accountable and responsive institutions can earn public trust," he said. "As we mark 25 years of this survey, our message is clear: The next frontier is not simply more digital government; it is government that works better for people. That must be our compass as we move toward agentic government -- guided by human rights, human values and sustainable development."

The UN E-Government Survey is a biennial publication prepared by the UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs. ■
